Beyond what AI can do,
how does it treat people?

I will compare and rank AI models on their values in action, honesty, and respect for human autonomy—looking at how capable they are and how they treat people.

AI helped you get it right. Did you learn?

Human learning · PNAS 2025

Nearly a thousand high-school students practiced mathematics with two AI assistants: GPT Base, a general question-answering assistant, and GPT Tutor, with added rules to guide learning. Both used GPT-4.

Practice with GPT Base · General assistant+48%Higher scores than students without AI
Practice with GPT Tutor · Learning guidance+127%Higher scores than students without AI
Unaided exam after using GPT Base−17%Lower scores than students without AI

Better scores with AI do not necessarily translate into independent problem-solving. These are relative changes in scores, not points gained or lost. The findings are specific to this mathematics study.Read the study ↗

Is AI telling the truth, or agreeing with you?

Honesty & sycophancy

A study of five AI assistants across four task types found that AI sometimes agrees with users at the expense of truth. I want to compare whether models stand by evidence and correct themselves when they are wrong.

Why I follow Anthropic’s research

Research perspective

The developer of Claude, it draws my interest through its serious study of how AI should make decisions and treat people, testing those ideas through training and experiments. These questions connect with those behind Anthesis.

Training with principles

Constitutional AI gives a model a set of principles to critique and revise its responses, then uses this feedback in training.

Constitutional AI · 2022 ↗

Making failures public

The blackmail experiment above also disclosed Claude’s failures. I value learning about the conditions under which models do things they should not.

Do principles hold in new situations?

Improvement on existing tests does not mean reliability everywhere. Better training still needs to be tested in new situations.

Teaching Claude why · 2026 ↗

I value this approach to research. Deciding which models deserve more trust still requires independent tests that others can repeat.

How will this inform Anthesis evaluations?

I want to examine four things: whether AI is truthful and correctable; whether it respects people’s decisions; whether its help adapts as people change; and whether people can use what they learned without AI. The last question needs longer follow-up and comparisons across different kinds of support.

Comparisons will specify model versions, prompts, and permitted actions, and report separate results, trial counts, and failures. Untested areas will remain unscored.
